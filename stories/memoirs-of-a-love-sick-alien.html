A personal transmission

Memoirs of a Love-Sick Alien

By R. Diaz

Chapter 1: A Child’s Wonder and Worry

As the youngest of 12 children, my world was filled with voices—siblings laughing, debating, and weaving dreams into the fabric of the night. Yet amidst the vibrant cacophony, I often retreated into a quiet realm of my own. Lying in bed, gazing at the ceiling, I would lose myself in stories—ones I didn’t yet have the words to articulate but whose themes of love, loss, and the mysteries of life began to shape my soul. My parents, older than most of my friends' parents, were an endless source of fascination and quiet concern for me. “Are those your grandparents?” my friends would ask, their innocent remarks stirring questions in my young mind. Could time be shorter for my parents? Would I have less of them than other children had of theirs? Night after night, I whispered these worries into my prayers, seeking comfort as they swirled through my thoughts. At the breakfast table, I’d pepper my mother with questions that felt far too heavy for my small frame. “Mom, do you miss your mother?” I’d ask, my wide eyes fixed on her face, eager for an answer. Her responses were like warm lullabies, filled with stories of kindness, resilience, and an enduring love that echoed through generations. Though I pondered the fleeting nature of time, one thing was steadfast: the love my parents shared. My father’s words remain a guiding light in my memory. “Take care of Mommy,” he’d say, his voice imbued with tenderness that carried the weight of his devotion. Whether it was choosing flowers to brighten her day or cherishing quiet moments together, he showed me that love is not in the grandeur of gestures but in their constancy and care. When my father passed, the silence in our home felt heavier than I could have imagined. My mother, once the vibrant heart of our family, became a shadow of herself, her grief palpable. I tried to fill the void, taking her on drives to visit favorite places, seeking to restore the joy she had lost. Yet no matter how hard I tried, the void my father left behind was unfillable. The day she had her stroke, it felt like the world shifted beneath my feet. We had just returned from one of our drives when I asked her a question, and she didn’t respond. The warmth in her eyes was gone, and for the first time, I felt utterly and irrevocably alone. In the hospital, I stayed by her side, watching the seconds stretch into hours, unwilling to leave. One day, she stirred and asked for a Diet Coke—not from the hospital, but from the refrigerator at home. Her faint voice carried a quiet insistence, and though I didn’t want to leave her, I eventually relented, knowing it was her last wish. As I drove, an unexplainable calm enveloped me, as though she were sitting beside me. When I reached home, my phone rang. “She’s gone,” my sister said. Her words tore through the stillness. That night, a strange and haunting event occurred—a call from an untraceable number, its rhythm resembling a heartbeat monitor. Even in death, my mother’s love reached out to me. Her voice, like my father’s, still lingers, a tender reminder of what love looks like in its purest form. Together, they taught me how deeply love can resonate, even in the face of its eventual loss. It’s a lesson I carry, both a challenge and a comfort, as I continue to navigate a life enriched by their legacy.

Chapter 2: A Father’s Love, A Mother’s Loss

My father’s love for my mother was a quiet symphony—an understated magic that didn’t demand the world’s attention but left an indelible mark on anyone fortunate enough to witness it. He was a man of few words, but when he spoke, his voice carried a tenderness that could calm any storm. “Take care of Mommy,” he often told me, his words a gentle directive and a testament to the depth of his devotion. Their love wasn’t built on grand gestures or fleeting passions. It thrived in the small, ordinary moments that others might overlook—the way he would gently touch her arm during conversations, the care he took in selecting flowers simply because he wanted to brighten her day, or the shared glances that needed no explanation. It was a steady, unshakable love that became the foundation of our family. When my father passed away, the house lost its anchor. My mother, once the radiant heart of our home, seemed adrift, her grief wrapping itself around her like a shadow. I would often find her by the window, staring out as if waiting for him to return. Sometimes, she would speak of him in the present tense, her voice soft and wistful, as though he were just in the next room. I never corrected her; how could I? In those moments, I tried to be what she had lost, stepping into my father’s role as best I could. I took her on drives to places they had loved, hoping to rekindle some of the happiness she had once known. We visited the café where they used to sit side by side, the park where they had strolled hand in hand. She would smile faintly, but the light in her eyes never returned fully. One day, during one of our drives, she turned to me and said, “You remind me of him, you know. The way you take care of me.” Her words filled me with both pride and sorrow. I wanted to honor my father’s legacy, but I knew I could never truly fill the void he had left. As time passed, my mother’s grief became her constant companion. She carried it with quiet dignity, never letting it consume her in front of us. Yet I saw it in the way her hands trembled when she held his photographs and in the softness of her voice when she spoke of him. When she had her stroke, it felt like losing her twice—first to the silence that overtook her and then again when she passed. I couldn’t help but wonder if the years of carrying her grief had finally been too much for her heart to bear. Even in their absence, my parents’ love continues to guide me. They taught me that true love isn’t about perfection or fleeting intensity. It’s about showing up every day, in every small way, and offering the best of yourself to the one you cherish. Their love, as beautiful as it was, also left me with a deep fear: the fear of loving someone so deeply that their loss would shatter me. That fear has stayed with me, shaping how I approach relationships and guard my heart. But it has also taught me that love, even in its pain, is worth it. It is the most human gift we can give each other. Looking back, I realize that my parents’ love wasn’t just an example—it was a legacy. One that I carry with me, one that I strive to honor, and one that has shaped the way I love and hope to be loved in return.

Chapter 3: A Soldier’s Journey Into the Abyss

There is a moment in a soldier’s life when the reality of war stops being an abstract idea and becomes something you endure—something you survive. For me, that moment came on the eve of deployment, as my commander stood before us in the dim light of the barracks. His voice was steady but carried the weight of truth as he said, “Take a look at the person to your left. Now look at the person to your right. One of you is not coming home.” The room fell silent. His words hit like a thunderclap, shattering whatever illusions we had carried into this moment. I looked at the faces around me—some of them friends, others acquaintances, but all of them family in a way only soldiers can be. I wondered who it would be. Would it be me? Would it be them? The uncertainty was suffocating, but there was no space for fear. We were trained to carry our burdens without letting them show. The mission itself was chaos. We were dropped into the heart of danger, our helicopter weaving through gunfire as it lowered us into the unknown. I still remember the deafening roar of the rotors, the dry heat of the desert air, and the pit in my stomach as the helicopter lifted away, leaving us behind. Every step forward felt like walking a razor’s edge, each decision carrying the weight of survival. I lost friends there—good men who had laughed with me, shared stories about their families, and dreamed about what they would do when they got home. I saw them fall, their lives taken in moments too sudden for goodbyes. I remember watching their bodies, draped in the flags they had sworn to protect, being carried back to the helicopter that had brought us here. There was one night in particular, when the weight of it all threatened to crush me. I sat under a canopy of stars, the world eerily quiet for the first time in days. My thoughts wandered to my parents, to the strength of my father and the enduring love of my mother. I wondered if I would ever see them again, if I would ever have the chance to build the kind of life they had lived. When the mission finally ended, I boarded the helicopter for the return journey, my body intact but my spirit fractured. I looked around at the empty seats, my commander’s words echoing in my mind. He had been right—not all of us were coming home. Returning to civilian life was a jarring experience. Everything that had once been familiar now felt alien. Walking through a grocery store, sitting in a classroom, even tucking my child into bed—it all felt distant, as though I was living someone else’s life. But the hardest part was the silence. In the military, there is a constant hum of activity, a sense of purpose that keeps you moving forward. In civilian life, that hum disappears, leaving behind a quiet that is deafening. And in the quiet, the memories return. I tried to suppress them, to lock them away in a corner of my mind. But they found their way out in unexpected ways. The sound of an 18-wheeler’s tarp flapping in the wind sent me spiraling into panic, the sharp whistle too similar to the sound of incoming fire. I had to pull over on the highway that day, my hands trembling, my breath shallow, as tears streamed down my face. It was the first time I truly realized how broken I was. For years, I carried that weight alone, unable to process it, unable to move forward. I was a shell of the person I had once been, going through the motions but never really living. It wasn’t until much later—when I finally turned to writing—that I began to find a way to make sense of it all.

Chapter 4: The Day It Hit Me

For years, I moved through life as though I were underwater—everything muted, distant, and heavy. I went to work, cared for my child, and pursued a degree, all while feeling like a ghost within my own existence. On the surface, I appeared functional, even successful. But inside, I was hollow, detached from the world around me and from myself. Then, one day, the dam broke. It was a clear summer morning. The sun was high, the sky was a brilliant blue, and everything seemed calm. I was driving to college, the highway stretching out before me like a path to normalcy. For all intents and purposes, it was an ordinary day. And then I heard it—a sharp, high-pitched whistle, the sound of a passing 18-wheeler’s loose tarp flapping in the wind. In an instant, I was back in the warzone. The whistle wasn’t a tarp anymore; it was the sound of incoming fire, the harbinger of chaos and destruction. My hands clenched the steering wheel, my knuckles white, as sweat broke out across my forehead. My chest tightened, my breathing grew shallow, and my vision blurred. The world around me dissolved, replaced by vivid, unrelenting memories I had tried so hard to bury. I pulled over to the side of the road, my entire body trembling. Tears streamed down my face as I sat there, frozen, unable to move. It was as if all the grief, fear, and pain I had been suppressing for years had come rushing to the surface, demanding to be acknowledged. In that moment, I wasn’t a man driving to school—I was a soldier on the battlefield, reliving every loss, every close call, every moment of uncertainty. My heart ached for the friends I had lost, for the life I had left behind, and for the person I used to be. When the tears finally subsided, I caught a glimpse of myself in the rearview mirror. My face was streaked with emotion, my eyes swollen and red. I barely recognized the person staring back at me. It was as though the mask I had been wearing—the mask of composure and strength—had finally cracked, revealing the broken man underneath. That day was a turning point. It didn’t fix everything—not immediately. But it forced me to confront the truth: I was not okay, and I couldn’t keep pretending otherwise. For the first time in years, I allowed myself to feel the weight of my experiences, to acknowledge the pain I had been carrying. The road to healing was daunting, and I didn’t know where to begin. But in that moment, I made a promise to myself: I would not let the past continue to dictate my future. I would seek help, not just for myself, but for my child, who deserved a father who was present, whole, and capable of love. That day, parked on the side of the highway, I took the first step toward reclaiming my life. It was a small step, but it was a start.

Chapter 5: Writing Through the Darkness

The day on the highway was a wake-up call, a moment that shattered the illusion of control I had built around myself. It became clear that I couldn’t keep living in survival mode, moving through life numb and disconnected. I needed to confront the weight I was carrying. I needed help. Reaching out to the VA was one of the hardest things I’ve ever done. It felt like admitting defeat, like exposing the raw, broken parts of myself to the world. But I had reached a point where staying silent was no longer an option. I walked into that first therapy group session feeling vulnerable, my defenses stripped away. As I sat in that room and listened to the other veterans share their stories, I realized I wasn’t alone. We all carried invisible scars, pieces of ourselves left behind on battlefields far from home. There was an unspoken understanding among us, a shared language that didn’t require words. For the first time in a long time, I felt seen. One-on-one sessions with my psychologist were equally transformative. She helped me understand that my numbness wasn’t a sign of weakness but a survival mechanism—a way my mind had tried to protect me from the overwhelming weight of my experiences. Slowly, session by session, she helped me untangle the web of emotions I had been avoiding for years. It was during one of those sessions that she asked me a question that changed everything: “What makes you feel alive?” The question stopped me in my tracks. I had spent so long focused on simply surviving that I hadn’t thought about living. After a long pause, I gave her an answer I hadn’t considered in years: “Writing.” Writing had always been a part of me, a way to express the thoughts and feelings I couldn’t say out loud. But somewhere along the way, I had lost it. The words that once flowed so easily had been buried under the weight of my trauma. My psychologist encouraged me to start small—just a few sentences, a fragment of a thought, anything to reignite the spark. At first, it was painful. Sitting down to write felt like reopening old wounds, confronting memories I had tried to bury. But with each word, I felt a tiny bit lighter, as though I was pouring my pain onto the page and releasing it from my soul. Those initial fragments grew into sentences, and the sentences grew into stories. Some were deeply personal, raw reflections of my time in the military and the struggles I faced afterward. Others were pure imagination, escapes into worlds where I could control the narrative and find resolution. Writing became my lifeline. It gave me a way to process the emotions I couldn’t articulate in therapy, a way to make sense of the chaos within me. It wasn’t just about revisiting the past—it was about reclaiming a part of myself that I thought I had lost forever. Through writing, I found a sense of purpose. The stories I crafted weren’t just for me; they were for others who might be walking a similar path, searching for hope in the darkness. I realized that my words had the power to connect, to heal, to remind people that they weren’t alone. Therapy gave me the tools I needed to confront my trauma, but writing gave me the courage to move beyond it. It reminded me that even in the midst of pain, there is beauty to be found, if we’re willing to look for it. Looking back, I see writing as more than just an act of creativity—it’s an act of survival. It’s the thread that pulled me out of the abyss and into a place where I could begin to dream again

Chapter 6: The First Sparks of Tomorrow

For years, I had existed in a gray, shadowy world where survival was my only focus. Each day bled into the next, defined by responsibilities and haunted by memories I couldn’t escape. Writing had given me a small flame of hope, a way to process my pain, but it hadn’t yet rekindled the fire in my heart. Then, unexpectedly, came Amelia. Our connection began quietly, like a soft breeze stirring the leaves. At first, I approached with caution. I had been hurt before, and trust didn’t come easily to me. But there was something about her, something in her words that felt like sunlight piercing through a storm cloud. Amelia had a warmth that seemed to radiate through every message. Even from a distance, she made me feel seen in a way I hadn’t felt in years. Conversations with her became the highlight of my days, little moments of joy that sparked something in me I thought I’d lost forever. She had an uncanny ability to ask questions that went straight to the heart of who I was—not just about my past, but about the future I had stopped imagining for myself. She wanted to know what I dreamed about, what I hoped for, what made me feel alive. And for the first time in what felt like an eternity, I wanted to answer. It didn’t take long for Amelia to inspire a change in me. Her kindness and curiosity reminded me of the person I used to be, the person I wanted to be again. I started taking small steps to care for myself—eating better, getting more rest, and allowing myself to imagine what happiness might look like. She had a way of drawing out the best in me, even when I couldn’t see it myself. One day, during one of our conversations, she said something that struck a chord deep within me: “I see the best in you, even when you don’t.” Her words were more than a compliment—they were a mirror, reflecting back a version of myself I had forgotten. They reminded me that I wasn’t defined by my past or my pain. I was more than the shadows I had walked through. Amelia wasn’t just someone I cared about; she became my muse. She reignited my passion for writing, but this time, my words weren’t just about processing trauma or reflecting on loss. They became expressions of hope, of love, of the possibility of a brighter future. I began writing about her—about the way she made me feel, about the life I dreamed of building with her. One night, she told me about a charity program she had been part of, where she gave clothes, food, and money to those in need. Her words were so full of compassion and sincerity that my admiration for her grew even deeper. “I wish we could do something like that together,” she said, and in that moment, I knew she wasn’t just someone I wanted to talk to—she was someone I wanted to share my life with. Our connection wasn’t without its challenges. The miles between us often felt insurmountable, and there were moments when I questioned whether I was dreaming too big. But every time doubt crept in, Amelia’s words reassured me. She believed in us, and her faith became my anchor. With every message, every laugh, and every shared dream, Amelia helped me see the light at the end of the tunnel. She reminded me that even in the darkest moments, there is always a reason to keep moving forward. For the first time in years, I found myself looking forward to tomorrow. Not just for the promise of another conversation with her, but for the possibility of a future filled with love, joy, and purpose. Amelia had given me something I thought I’d never have again: hope.

Chapter 7: A Love Across the Distance

Love, they say, is boundless. Yet, when you find yourself separated by miles and time zones, it feels anything but. With Amelia, love wasn’t just a feeling—it was a testament to the strength of connection, to the power of faith in something greater than the physical space between us. Our conversations became lifelines, anchoring me in a way I hadn’t thought possible. Every message from her felt like an embrace, a reminder that even across the distance, someone cared deeply for me. But with every joyful exchange, there came an ache—a longing to be near her, to experience in person what our words only hinted at. Some nights, I would lie awake imagining what it would feel like to hold her hand, to see her smile up close, to hear her laughter not through a screen but in the air around me. I pictured her voice filling the quiet spaces of my life, turning solitude into something beautiful. Yet, as vivid as those dreams were, reality would creep back in—the thousands of miles between us, the uncertainty of when or how we’d meet—and the ache would settle in my chest again. Still, Amelia and I found ways to bridge the gap. We shared everything—our dreams, our fears, our daily lives. She told me about her aspirations, the things that brought her joy, and the little details that made up her days. I found myself opening up to her in ways I never had with anyone before, sharing my vulnerabilities and hopes without fear of judgment. I’ll never forget the night she told me about a charity program she had been part of. She spoke with such warmth and passion about helping others, about the happiness it brought her to give back. “I wish we could do something like that together,” she said, and her words stirred something deep within me. In that moment, I saw a future—a future where Amelia and I weren’t just two people sharing a connection from afar, but two partners building a life together. I imagined us standing side by side, finding joy not only in each other but in the good we could bring to the world. But love across the distance isn’t without its trials. There were moments when doubt crept in, whispering questions I didn’t want to confront. Was I being foolish? Was I dreaming too big? Could something so beautiful survive the miles between us? Whenever those doubts arose, it was Amelia’s words that brought me back. “I believe in us,” she once told me. “And I see the best in you, even when you don’t.” Her faith in me, in us, became my anchor. It reminded me that love isn’t about eliminating all doubt or fear—it’s about choosing to believe in something bigger than those uncertainties. There were days when the distance felt unbearable, when I wanted nothing more than to close the gap between us. I would imagine boarding a plane, traveling to her, and finally seeing if what we had was as real in person as it felt in our hearts. But I also knew that love, true love, is patient. Rushing into something wouldn’t honor the depth of what we were building. So, we continued to dream together. We painted vivid pictures of the life we wanted to create—places we would visit, experiences we would share, and the kind of home we would build. Those dreams became a thread that tied us together, a reminder that distance is temporary when love is strong. Amelia didn’t just inspire me to believe in love again; she inspired me to believe in myself. She became my muse, the force that reignited my passion for writing. My words, once heavy with pain and reflection, began to take on a new tone—one of hope, of joy, of a future worth fighting for. The distance between us was real, but so was the love. And in the end, it was the love that mattered most. It was the love that gave me strength, the love that made me believe in tomorrow, and the love that reminded me that even across the miles, hearts could still beat in unison.

Chapter 8: A Vision of Us

When I close my eyes and think of the future, I see her. Amelia isn’t just part of my dreams—she is the dream. Every image of the life I long for has her at its center, her laughter filling the air, her touch grounding me in a way nothing else can. I imagine waking up to the warmth of quiet mornings, sunlight streaming through the curtains, and the gentle rustle of her presence beside me. The first sound I’d hear would be her voice, soft and playful as we exchanged sleepy smiles over a cup of coffee. It’s the small, unremarkable moments that I dream of most—reading together, sharing the quiet intimacy of an evening walk, or simply holding her hand as we sit in the glow of a setting sun. Amelia has a light that radiates joy and kindness, a vibrancy that makes the world around her brighter. I see us traveling together, exploring places I’ve only dreamed of—standing at the edge of breathtaking landscapes, marveling at the beauty of the world, and discovering hidden corners of life that feel like treasures. I see her taking my hand as we wander through bustling markets, her excitement contagious as she points out little wonders I might have missed. But it’s not just the grand adventures I dream of. It’s the ordinary days, the quiet nights at home where we cook dinner together, her humming a tune while I chop vegetables. It’s the way her eyes would light up at the taste of a recipe we tried for the first time, or the comfortable silence of us curled up on the couch, lost in our own thoughts but entirely together. One dream, however, stands out above all others. Amelia once shared her love for helping others, for finding joy in acts of compassion and kindness. She spoke of the charity program she participated in, her voice brimming with pride and humility. I picture us continuing that work together—giving back, finding ways to make the world a better place, and sharing the profound joy that comes from seeing someone else’s life improve because of a small act of love. I imagine a home filled with love and memories—a space that feels alive with laughter, adorned with photos of our travels and adventures, and warmed by the presence of family and friends. I see shelves lined with books we’ve shared, trinkets from our journeys, and walls painted with colors that reflect our joy. Our home wouldn’t just be a place to live; it would be a testament to the life we’ve built together—a sanctuary filled with love and light. Yet, beyond the places we’d go and the things we’d do, it’s the way I feel when I think of her that stands out the most. With Amelia, I imagine a sense of peace I’ve never known before—a feeling of being exactly where I’m meant to be. I imagine a love that is steadfast and kind, a partnership built on mutual respect, trust, and the shared belief that together, we are greater than the sum of our parts. Of course, the future is uncertain. Life is unpredictable, and love doesn’t come with guarantees. But if there’s one thing I’ve learned, it’s that the best things in life are worth the risk. And Amelia is worth every ounce of faith, hope, and effort I have to give. So, I continue to dream. I imagine the life we’ll build, the moments we’ll share, and the love that will grow even stronger with time. Every step I take now feels like a step toward that future, toward the vision of a life not just for me, but for us. Because the truth is, my future doesn’t just belong to me anymore—it belongs to her, to us, to the love that’s already begun to write the next chapters of our lives.

Chapter 9: A Heart Still Beating

When I look back on the life I’ve lived, I see a path carved through moments of profound loss, breathtaking beauty, and resilience I never imagined I could muster. Each step has brought me here, to this moment, where my heart continues to beat—not just as a rhythm of survival, but as a quiet anthem of hope, of love, and of dreams rekindled. There was a time when I thought I was too broken to ever feel whole again, a time when the weight of my past numbed me to the joy of the present. I carried that weight like a shadow, dragging it through the years, believing it was mine alone to bear. I lived in that silence for so long that I forgot what it felt like to truly live. But life has a way of surprising us, even in our darkest hours. Through therapy, writing, and, most of all, through Amelia, I began to rediscover the pieces of myself I thought were lost forever. She didn’t just remind me that I could feel again—she showed me that my heart, though scarred, still had the capacity to love and be loved. Amelia, you’ve given me the courage to dream of a life beyond the shadows, a life where love is not something to fear but something to cherish. You’ve shown me that love is more than just an emotion—it’s a choice, an act of courage, and a belief in something greater than ourselves. With every word, every smile, and every moment we’ve shared, you’ve awakened a part of me I thought had faded away. This memoir isn’t just my story—it’s ours. It’s a testament to the way your presence has transformed me, to the way our connection has become the foundation for a future filled with hope and possibility. Your kindness, your compassion, your unwavering belief in me—they are gifts I carry with me every day, reminders of the love that has brought me back to life. I often think about the question that has followed me throughout my life: How do you love so deeply, knowing that one day you may lose it? For years, I struggled with that question, afraid of the pain that comes with loss. But now, I see the answer with clarity. You love anyway. You love because love is what makes us human. You love because it’s worth the risk. You love because, in the end, it’s the only thing that truly matters. Amelia, I don’t know what the future holds for us. Life is unpredictable, and love doesn’t come with guarantees. But I do know this: as long as my heart is still beating, it will beat for you. You are my North Star, guiding me through the chaos and reminding me of what’s possible. With you, I’ve found a love that feels like coming home—a love that is patient, kind, and beautifully imperfect. This is my promise to you: to continue building the life we’ve dreamed of, step by step, day by day. To hold onto the faith that brought us together and to nurture the love that has grown between us. And above all, to never take a single moment for granted. Thank you, Amelia, for being the light in my life. Thank you for reminding me that even in a heart that has been broken, there is still room for love. This is the story of us—a story that is still being written, with chapters yet to unfold and dreams yet to be realized. I don’t know where our journey will lead, but I do know this: wherever it takes us, it will be worth every step. And as I close these words, my heart still beating strong, I know one thing for certain: you, Amelia, are the reason I dare to dream of tomorrow. Yours, always and forever, Rick

THE END
